– The minimum elevation to be used is 20 degrees.
– Model of an average beam size contour at the 7 dB falloff, i.e. not overlap any beams
more than their 7 dB main lobe contour of each beam (no further beam overlap to avoid
self-interference);
NOTE: This has to be used in conjunction with the pattern in recommends 1.4 in Recommendation

– Prioritize placing beams with the lowest scan angle / path length;
– Distribute the beam count evenly among eligible satellites over the service area - Equal

– The simulation/service area can be divided into cells and for each cell a beam is
assigned to it from one of the eligible satellites.
The following interference management approaches have been shown to potentially facilitate
compliance with established regulatory limits to protect incumbent services operating in
neighboring countries.
In terms of interference management in the vicinity of borders:
– When a beam is placed right before the border or in its vicinity, select, among available
satellites, those flying above the country where the D2C service is being provided. This
will maximize the impact of the atmospheric attenuation and spreading loss for the
antenna side lobes in the direction of the country where the victim receiver is located.
– Adjust the main beam pfd close to the border, i.e. use a lower main beam pfd when
transmitting close to the border in order to manage cross-border aggregate interference

In-beam PFD modelling options
In terms of modelling the variation of PFD with slant path, several options can be considered, and
correspond to several ways operations can occur:
– Keep PFD constant on the ground, i.e. adjust eirp at the satellite to ensure a constant
PFD on the ground regardless of the slant path / arrival angle
– Keep eirp constant at the satellite: this implies that the PFD value contained in Table 1
is only ensured at Nadir, while it will be lower at lower elevation, i.e. as the slant path
increases
– Hybrid mode: some systems adjust the eirp to ensure constant PFD on the ground only
up to a certain elevation angle, after which the eirp is not increased more, thus the PFD
decreases as the elevation angle decreases

Adjacent Channel Spatial Correlation Factor
In this section, using basic antenna and PA non-linear model theory, we present a study of the
spatial non-linear correlation factors in the adjacent channel. Simulations using multiple array sizes
confirm that these correlation factors drop rapidly with beam count. A beam-count >= 20 is
required to maintain a correlation factor <= 0.1 Subsequently, the element pattern is appropriate for
use with out-of-band emissions in the adjacent channel for digital beamforming arrays with large
number beams. For such arrays, it is shown that the EIRP at the p’th adjacent channel can be

3.1.2.4 Modelling of operations
Cross-border interference management
From a cross-border interference management point of view, this system controls beam
transmission within a targeted geographical area for each individual beam including the beam
on/off and the transmit power. The system has the capacity to adjust beam forming to compensate
for the satellite’s movement as it orbits overhead within range of an intended service area such that
each individual beam will track an assigned cell on ground. This real-time calculation, application,
and control of the phased array is always running on-board the satellite, providing the engineering
solution to optimally operate RF transmissions where geographically intended and thereby reducing
transmission in targeted protection areas, e.g., international borders.
As such, cross-border interference will be managed and mitigated by several key system
components:
(1) beam forming for sharp roll off near the border,
(2) beam power management for the beams servicing the cells near the border, and
(3) turning off the cells next to the border if necessary.]

[Modelling of in-band and out-of-band DC-MSS-IMT satellite antenna radiation patterns
The following definition is employed to characterise the terms in-band and out-of-band:
– “In-band” as the frequency range covered by the (desired) channel bandwidth; and
– “Out-of-band” as the frequency range falling immediately outside the channel
bandwidth, up to ±10 MHz from the operating band lower and upper edges.
The MSS NGSO satellites under consideration are assumed to be part of a constellation of satellites,
capable of transmitting multiple beams per-satellite that are electrically steerable from the satellite
position in orbit. From this, operate with phased array antennas to illuminate a finite area on the
Earth’s surface (in units of km2). Since the array factor of the phased array antenna relies on
coherent combining of signals, it is not expected that the phased array will operate in the same
manner outside the in-band frequency range. Therefore, the out-of-band radiation characteristics of
phased arrays will differ relative to its in-band characteristics. However, from the physics of phased
array antennas, the out-of-band pattern should have a degree of correlation, denoted by ρ, with the
in-band pattern. The exact value of ρ, (capturing the degree of this correlation) primarily depends
on three factors:
1) The amount of frequency separation relative to the in-band frequency lower and upper
edges;
2) The RF design/architecture of the phased array antenna, including any power dividers,
phase shifters and power combiner circuits; and
3) The design/architecture of the satellite’s RF up(/down)-conversion chains serving as a
feed network to phased array antenna with appropriate bandpass filtering to meet the
desired ACLR levels.
The phased array antenna at the NGSO satellites will actively control the individual signals being
fed to each antenna element in the array to shape and direct the antenna beam(s) towards the wanted
area on the Earth’s surface. The net radiated out-of-band emissions (with contributions from both
the phased array and its RF up(/down)-conversion chains) are then primarily governed by the RF
up-converter’s out-of-band response due to the RF mixing process, intermodulation products due to
power amplifier non-linearities, as well as the net phased array antenna’s impedance and
polarisation mismatches, respectively. Two important cases worth drawing out are:
 If ρ=0, the correlation level is exactly zero, in which case the out-of-band antenna
pattern bears no resemblance to the in-band antenna pattern; and even in this case, one
cannot conclude that the composite in-band phased array pattern collapses to an antenna
pattern of a single-element for out-of-band frequencies.
 For all other cases, 0<ρ≤1, the level of correlation is non-zero (bounded by a
maximum value of 1), in which case the out-of-band antenna pattern would be
correlated to the in-band antenna pattern, as governed by ρ.

[Preliminary studies demonstrate the following key aspects:
• D2C systems can use mitigation techniques to meet applicable aggregate cross-border
PFD limits to protect UEs. In sharing studies, modelling these techniques is
fundamental in order to accurately reflect real operations. Not modelling these
techniques will inevitably, and unjustifiably, result in studies showing impossibility to
meet applicable limits when envisaged D2C systems use the intended in-country PFD
levels to offer service;
• Protection of UEs can be ensured by considering aggregate PFD; while on the contrary,
attempting to reverse engineer single-entry (i.e., per satellite) PFD values to be applied
to each satellite in this system leads to inefficient spectrum use and unnecessary
constraints on D2C systems. Each system, based on its technology and emission
management capabilities, will be able to meet applicable limits in different ways, with
the manner to achieve this depending on the individual systems and their technologies;
• Aggregation of interference from multiple systems, when mitigation techniques are
implemented for each of the systems, is not an issue at all, even when analysing
localized worst-case situations, i.e., areas attached to borders of different countries
where D2C services are operational. More studies are certainly needed in this respect,
but these initial results are definitely encouraging.]
